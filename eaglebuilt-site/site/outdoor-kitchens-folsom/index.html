Four Folsom, not one

Empire Ranch and Broadstone. Pools, established streets, committees that have seen a lot of patio covers. The island is usually joining a yard that is already landscaped, so the finish has to sit next to what is there.

Folsom Ranch. South of 50, newer, and the yards are smaller. A committee is normal. The useful drawing is a straight or short L that respects the cover, not a pavilion borrowed from a half-acre lot.

The canyon. Grade, access, and wind down the American River. Stone and concrete handle the sun. The layout has to handle the hill.

Historic Folsom. Tighter lots, older walls, and less room to stage a pour. Worth saying out loud before anyone talks about a 12-foot island.

HOA notes are general. Empire Ranch, Broadstone, and Folsom Ranch each have their own rules about structures and colors. Ask that committee what they want to see. Nothing here is their approval, and nothing here is a refusal.

Questions

Folsom questions

Who permits a BBQ island in Folsom?

The City of Folsom. It is Sacramento County geographically and its own building division for a permit. Gas, electrical, plumbing, and anything with a roof are the usual reasons they get involved. The guide is the checklist of questions. It is not the city's worksheet.

Folsom Ranch yards are smaller than Empire Ranch. Does the island change?

The equipment can be the same and the run usually cannot. A Folsom Ranch lot often has a modest cover and a committee that measures what you add. Empire Ranch and Broadstone more often have a pool and room for an L or a bar that faces the water. Briggs Ranch sits between those, older and more settled. The drawing starts from the cover and the setback you actually have.

The canyon lots slope toward the river. Does that matter?

Yes. American River Canyon North and South, and parts of the historic side of Folsom, drop away from the house. A level counter on a falling grade means the base of the island is not a simple rectangle, and access for a concrete pour is the first conversation, not the veneer. Historic Folsom lots near Sutter Street are a different problem again: tight, and sometimes already built to the setback. Say which part of Folsom it is when you call.
