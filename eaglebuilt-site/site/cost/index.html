Pricing

How much does an outdoor kitchen cost?

Two numbers decide it: the island you build and the equipment you put in it. We price them separately, because one of them barely moves and the other can triple your total.

What these numbers do and don't cover

Included

Block structure, colored concrete countertop, stucco finish, equipment mounting, the labor to build it, and haul-off at the end.

Not included

Running new gas or electrical service to the island location, plumbing supply and drain, permits, slab or footing work, upgraded countertop material, and anything the site throws up once we open it.

These are budgetary figures. They are real rates and real catalog prices, not padded estimates — but a firm quote follows a site measure. Access, grade, existing utilities and jurisdiction all move the number, and none of them are visible from a drawing.

Questions

Common cost questions

Why is equipment quoted separately from construction?

Because it is the variable that moves the total most. The island itself barely changes in price whether you put a $1,900 grill in it or a $9,500 one. Splitting the two quotes shows you exactly what a tier change costs before you commit to it.

What does stone veneer add?

$35 per square foot over the base rate. On a 10-foot island that is roughly $2,600. It is the single biggest change you can make to how the island looks, and the designer prices it live so you can see it against the stucco version.

Is a bigger island cheaper per foot?

Not materially. The linear-foot rate holds across sizes; what changes is the equipment mounting charge, which steps up on larger islands. The real cost driver is what goes in the bays, not how many feet of block you run.

Can I build it myself from your drawings?

Yes. The designer exports a plan, a dimensioned front elevation and a bay schedule listing every model number with the cutout to frame. That is enough for any competent mason, whether or not we do the work.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on your jurisdiction and which utilities are involved. A freestanding island on an existing slab often needs none; new gas, electrical or plumbing usually does. Our permits guide covers what tends to trigger one.
