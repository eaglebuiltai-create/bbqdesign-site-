Outdoor kitchen dimensions

The numbers that decide whether your island works: counter height, island depth, framing between cutouts, and the real published cutout for every component we carry.

The four numbers that matter

37.5"

Counter height

Finished top. The fridge sets it — 34.5" of clear opening underneath, plus a 3" poured slab.

32"

Island depth

Our standard, and it takes every cutout we carry. 24" is a back bar — no BBQ fits it.

42"

Raised bar

Height for a seating counter, typically 12 inches deep with a knee overhang.

2"

Framing gap

Structure between adjacent cutouts. Why a 30.6" opening occupies about 33" of island.

Questions

Common dimension questions

How deep should the island be?

32 inches is our standard. 31 of the 124 components we carry need a cutout deeper than 23.5 inches, which rules them out of a 24-inch island entirely. The saving on block is small; the loss of options is not.

How wide should a BBQ island be?

Width follows what goes in it. A 32-inch grill needs a 30 to 31 inch opening plus framing either side, so that bay alone is about 33 inches. A grill plus one storage bay is roughly six feet; add a refrigerator and a sink and you are at 10 to 12 feet.

Why does the finished island measure more than the cutouts add up to?

Two inches of structure sits between adjacent cutouts, plus the end walls. Four bays carry three internal gaps — six inches of block that has to go somewhere. The designer adds this allowance for you and shows the finished length as you build.

How accurate are these dimensions?

Every cutout comes from the manufacturer's published specification. Always confirm against the installation manual that ships with your appliance before cutting masonry — manufacturers revise dimensions between product generations, and some dealer listings disagree with the manufacturer.
