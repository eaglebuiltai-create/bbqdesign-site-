The part that saves money

It tells you when something won't fit

Because the designer knows every appliance's real cutout, it checks your island as you build it — and speaks up before anyone cuts block.

Put a 27½″ triple drawer under a grill and it will tell you only 24½″ of head-room is left. Spec a 24″ refrigerator on a 36″ counter and it will point out the opening needs 35⅛″ and the counter has to come up. Choose a professional-series grill on a 24″ deep island and it will tell you the cutout is deeper than the island.

These are the errors that turn into a demolition day. They are much cheaper to catch on a screen.

Worth knowing before you frame

Two things we found while building the catalog:

RCS Cutlass Pro cutouts run wider than the nominal size. The "30 inch" RON30A needs a 33″ opening; the 42″ needs 45″. Frame to the nominal size and the grill will not drop in.

Manufacturers and dealers sometimes disagree. Blaze lists different combo cutout heights than some dealer sites do for the same SKU. We carry the manufacturer's figure and tell you to verify against the installation manual.

Questions

Outdoor kitchen design, answered

Is the design tool really free?

Yes. Designing costs nothing and there is no software to install — it runs in your browser. We ask for your details so we can send your drawings and an itemized quote, and so a designer can answer questions about your build.

What is a good size for an outdoor kitchen?

A grill plus one storage bay is roughly 6 feet. Add a refrigerator and a sink and you are at 10 to 12 feet, which suits most backyards. Build 30″ deep rather than 24″ if you want a pizza oven or a professional-series grill — several of those cutouts are more than 23″ deep and will not fit a 24″ island. Leave 36 to 42 inches of walking room behind the cook.

How wide should a BBQ island be?

Width is driven by what goes in it. A 32″ grill needs a 30 to 31 inch opening plus framing either side, so that bay alone is about 33″. The designer adds the framing allowance for you and shows the finished length as you build, so you can size the island to the space you have rather than guessing.

How much does an outdoor kitchen cost?

We build CMU islands with a colored concrete countertop and stucco finish at a fixed rate per linear foot, with labor included. Equipment is quoted separately, because grills range from around $1,500 for a value-tier unit to over $10,000 for a luxury one — that single choice moves the total more than anything else. The designer shows both numbers live as you build. More on what an outdoor kitchen costs →

Which grill brands are included?

Blaze, Coyote, RCS, Summerset, Fire Magic, DCS, Alfresco, Lynx, Twin Eagles and Hestan — 124 components in total, each carrying its manufacturer's published cutout dimensions. You can mix brands in a single island. See the cutout dimensions →

Do I need a permit to build an outdoor kitchen?

It depends on your jurisdiction and what utilities are involved. A freestanding island on an existing slab often needs no permit, but running a new gas line, adding electrical circuits or plumbing a sink usually does. We handle permitting on the builds we do, and we can tell you what your city requires before you start.

Can I take the drawings to my own contractor?

Yes. The tool exports a plan view, a dimensioned front elevation and a 3D view, plus a bay schedule listing every model number with the exact opening to frame and its height above the slab. That is enough for any competent mason to build from, whether or not we do the work.

How accurate are the cutout dimensions?

Every cutout comes from the manufacturer's published specification, and each brand page shows when we last checked it. Always confirm against the installation manual that ships with your appliance before cutting masonry — manufacturers revise dimensions between product generations, and some dealer listings disagree with the manufacturer.
